High-Low Hierarchies with Intrinsic Rewards (HIRO)
The first step toward an automated HRL solution is to split the policy. A low-level
policy is responsible for interacting with the environment through states and actions,
as usual, but it does not receive the reward. A high-level policy suggests a high-level
action and rewards the low-level policy with a reward that is intrinsic to the requested
goal. When the environment produces a reward, it is given the high-level policy.

For example, the high-level policy would tell the low-level policy to attempt to move
to a specific state, and reward the low-level policy according to how close it gets. The
high-level policy samples at a slower rate than the low-level policy to provide a tem‐
poral abstraction.
You can parameterize the low-level reward function in any way you like. But
researchers propose that a distance-to-state reward helps speed up learning.
